# Sources for the README argument

Researched 9 November 2026. These sources support the proposed definition of
good as **cooperation under tension**. They were found while finalising the
README; do not describe them as reading undertaken earlier in development.
The student identified the two games as influences. The applications below
are proposed interpretations for this project, not claims made by the sources.

## 1. Communication, stress and fair failure

Ben Kane (Steel Crate Games), *Designing Asymmetric Gameplay for Keep Talking
and Nobody Explodes*, GDC 2016.

- [Talk page and abstract](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1023471/Designing-Asymmetric-Gameplay-For-Keep)
- [Author's slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2016/Presentations/Kane_Ben_Designing_Asymmetric_Gameplay.pdf)

The abstract identifies communication as the central experience. The slides
develop that argument: unfamiliar symbols and multitasking create communication
challenges (PDF page 25), then stress is added (page 26). Page 30 says failure
should arise from an avoidable mistake and conversation should concern player
strategy rather than deciphering designer intent. Pages 32–33 pair difficult
decisions with easy inputs and clear rules.

**Possible application:** the ship should pressure players to coordinate, while
controls and consequences remain understandable. A confusing interface is a
poor substitute for a difficult cooperative decision. This is a design
principle, not evidence that this game has achieved it.

## 2. Suspense, distinct roles and the risk of an idle partner

Joel Couture, *Road to the IGF: Total Mayhem Games' We Were Here*, 5 February
2018. Interview with writer Lucia De Visser and designer Benjamin Van Hemert.

[Read the interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/road-to-the-igf-total-mayhem-games-i-we-were-here-i-)

This is primary evidence through the developers' own answers. They identify
*Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes* as an influence, describe the appeal of
explaining observations a partner cannot see, and connect suspense with more
difficult and exciting puzzles. Van Hemert also describes the difficulty of
keeping both players stressed and challenged: sometimes one had less to do.
Their communication prototypes used physical rooms and walkie-talkies.

**Possible application:** different information should give each role a
meaningful contribution. Judging cooperation means checking whether both
players stay involved, not merely whether the pair can complete a puzzle.
This source supports identifying a design trade-off; it does not establish
that our balance or pacing is successful.

## 3. The direct influence: information divided under time pressure

Steel Crate Games, *Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes*, official game page.

[Official description](https://keeptalkinggame.com/)

The description divides access between the player looking at the bomb and
experts consulting a manual who cannot see it. The group must communicate to
defuse it before time expires. It also explicitly permits remote play using
the players' preferred voice-chat service.

**Possible application:** describe this game's influence precisely: separate
observations and instructions make communication necessary, while danger
makes that communication urgent. Using an external voice channel has an
established precedent, though our reason for choosing it must be our own.

## Optional reference for the original We Were Here

Total Mayhem Games, [official original-game page](https://www.wewereheregames.com/games/we-were-here).

The original game separates a Librarian and an Explorer and requires clear
voice communication over walkie-talkies. Useful for linking the named game;
the developer interview above offers a stronger argument than this product
description. No claims about later sequels are needed.

## Suggested short formulation to adapt

*Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes* makes divided information urgent through
time pressure. *We Were Here's* developers similarly connect explaining what
a partner cannot see with suspense, while acknowledging the difficulty of
keeping both roles involved. I want that tension aboard this ship: each
player must contribute observations and decisions. Following Kane's emphasis
on clear rules and avoidable mistakes, the challenge should come from
coordinating under pressure, with consequences players can understand.
